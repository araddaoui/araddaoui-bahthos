Student Wellbeing and Learning Modality Survey
Regional Higher Education Research Consortium, 2023

Overview:
This survey collected self-reported data from 1,200 undergraduate students
across six universities regarding their subjective experience of remote
versus in-person learning during the 2022-2023 academic year. Unlike
grade-based studies, this survey focused on wellbeing, motivation, and
perceived learning quality rather than measured academic outcomes.

Key findings:
62% of respondents reported feeling more anxious about their academic
performance under remote learning, citing uncertainty about whether they
were "keeping up" without direct instructor feedback. However, 71% also
reported preferring the flexibility of remote learning for personal
scheduling reasons, particularly students who were also employed.

Notably, students did not report a clear consensus on whether remote
learning improved or worsened their actual understanding of course
material — responses were nearly evenly split, with a slight lean toward
"no significant difference" (38%) over either "better" (31%) or "worse"
(29%).

Limitations:
This is self-reported perception data, not a measure of academic
performance, and should not be used to draw conclusions about grades or
test scores. The consortium notes this survey was designed to complement,
not replace, outcome-based studies on the same population.

Recommendation:
The consortium suggests that future research separate the question of
"does remote learning work" from "how do students feel about remote
learning," as this data suggests the two are not strongly correlated.
